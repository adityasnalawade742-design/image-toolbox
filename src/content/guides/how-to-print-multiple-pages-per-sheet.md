Printing several pages on one sheet, sometimes called "N-up" printing, saves paper and makes handy handouts: lecture
slides, a draft to proofread, a long report to read on the train. Most print dialogs can do it, and when they can't, or
when you need a file rather than a printout, you can make a new PDF with the pages already arranged.

## How small will the text be?

Every page is shrunk to fit its share of the sheet, so the main question is whether you can still read it. For A4 or
Letter pages printed on paper of the same size:

| Pages per sheet | Each page is printed at about | 12 pt text becomes about | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 70% | 8.5 pt | Reading and proofreading, easily readable |
| 4 | 50% | 6 pt | Handouts and drafts, readable up close |
| 6 or 8 | 35% | 4 pt | Overviews and slides with large text |
| 9 | 33% | 4 pt | Checking layout, not reading |
| 16 | 25% | 3 pt | Seeing a whole document's layout at a glance |

Slides are different: they are wide and use large text, so they shrink well. Six slides per sheet is the classic
lecture handout, and two per sheet keeps every word readable.

## In Adobe Acrobat Reader

1. Open the PDF and choose Print.
2. Under **Page Sizing & Handling**, click **Multiple**.
3. Choose the pages per sheet (2, 4, 6, 9, 16 or a custom grid) and the page order.
4. Tick **Print page border** if you want a thin line around each page.

## In Chrome or Edge

1. Open the PDF in the browser and press Ctrl+P (Cmd+P on a Mac).
2. Open **More settings**.
3. Set **Pages per sheet** to 2, 4, 6, 9 or 16.

## On a Mac (Preview and most apps)

1. Choose File › Print.
2. Open the **Layout** section of the print dialog. If you don't see it, click Show Details.
3. Set **Pages per Sheet**, and if you like, the layout direction and a border.

## In Word

Word has the setting on its own print screen: File › Print, then change **1 Page Per Sheet** under Settings to the
number you want. Windows printer drivers often have a similar "pages per sheet" option under Printer Properties too.

## When to make a new PDF instead

The print setting only affects that one printout. Making a new PDF with the pages already arranged is better when:

- **You want to share it.** Send the handout as a file and everyone prints it the same way.
- **A print shop or a copier prints it.** They print the file as it is, at actual size.
- **You print from a phone.** Phone print screens usually don't have a pages-per-sheet option.
- **You want control over the look.** Spacing between pages, borders and the reading order.

[N-up PDF](/n-up-pdf) does this in the browser, without uploading the file:

1. Open [N-up PDF](/n-up-pdf) and add your PDF.
2. Choose 2, 4, 6, 8, 9 or 16 pages per sheet.
3. Choose the paper: the same size as your pages, or A4, Letter or A3.
4. Choose whether pages run across or down, the spacing between them, and thin borders if you want them.
5. Download the new PDF and print it at actual size.

The sheet is turned automatically to whichever way shows the pages largest, so two portrait pages sit side by side on
a landscape sheet, and wide slides stack on a portrait one. Pages stay as sharp vector content: text is not turned into
a picture, so it prints crisply even when small.

If your pages are in several files, put them into one with [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf) first.

## Printing on both sides

Combining both is the biggest saving: 4 pages per sheet, printed on both sides, puts 8 pages on one sheet of paper. In
the print dialog, choose two-sided printing and **flip on long edge** for portrait sheets or **flip on short edge** for
landscape ones, so the back isn't upside down.

## Pages per sheet or a booklet?

They are easy to confuse:

- **Pages per sheet** keeps the pages in reading order across each sheet: 1 and 2 on the first sheet, 3 and 4 on the
  next. Good for handouts and reading.
- **A booklet** puts the pages in a special order so that when you print on both sides, fold the stack in half and
  staple it, it reads like a small book. Use [Booklet Maker](/booklet-maker) for that.
