A contact sheet is a page of small prints of a whole set of photos, laid out in rows, so you can see them all at once,
compare them side by side and pick the ones worth keeping. Photographers have used them for about a hundred years, and
they are still the quickest way to review a shoot, show a client a set to choose from, or keep a printed index of an
archive.

## Where the name comes from

With film, a photographer couldn't see the pictures until the roll was developed, and then only as small negatives. To
look at them properly, the strips of negatives were laid directly on a sheet of photo paper, pressed flat under glass,
and exposed to light. Because the negatives were in contact with the paper, each print came out exactly the size of
the negative: a whole roll of 35 mm film, 36 frames, fitted on one sheet of 8 × 10 inch paper.

Photographers went over these sheets with a magnifying loupe and a grease pencil, circling the frames to enlarge and
crossing out the rest. Marked-up contact sheets from famous shoots are now collected and published, because they show
every frame the photographer took, not just the one that became famous.

Today nothing touches the paper, but the name stayed. A contact sheet is now usually a printed page or a PDF of
thumbnails, often with each file name underneath, made from digital photos or film scans.

## What people use contact sheets for

| Use | Why a contact sheet helps |
|---|---|
| Choosing photos after a shoot | You see the whole set at once and spot near-duplicates, closed eyes and the strongest frames faster than by clicking through files |
| Showing a client the shots | They mark the ones they want on paper or in the PDF, using the file names, and you edit only those |
| Product and stock catalogues | One page shows the whole range, with the file name of each picture |
| Archives and film scans | A printed index of each folder or roll, kept with the prints or negatives |
| Casting and modelling | Agencies and actors send a sheet of a whole session instead of dozens of separate files |
| School and art projects | A record of every photo taken for a project, which some courses ask for |

## How to make a contact sheet for free

[Contact Sheet Maker](/contact-sheet-maker) builds the page for you in the browser. There is nothing to install, and
the photos are not uploaded.

1. Open [Contact Sheet Maker](/contact-sheet-maker) and add your photos. You can drag them into a different order.
2. Choose the paper: A4 or Letter, portrait or landscape.
3. Choose how many photos go in each row, from 2 to 8.
4. Leave file names on if you want to refer to photos by name, and add a title, such as the date or the client's name.
5. Download a PDF of all pages, or each page as a PNG or JPG.

Each photo is shown whole, never cropped, so portrait and landscape pictures sit side by side. When there are more photos
than fit on one page, more pages are added. Pages are made at 300 DPI, the usual resolution for printing.

### How many photos per row?

The more photos per row, the more fit on a page, but the smaller each one is. On an A4 page in portrait, roughly:

| Photos per row | Thumbnail width | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | about 6 cm | Showing a client, where faces and expressions need to be visible |
| 4 | about 4.5 cm | Most shoots: a good balance between size and photos per page |
| 6 | about 3 cm | Long shoots, product ranges and archive indexes |
| 8 | about 2 cm | Finding a picture in a large set, rather than judging it |

In landscape, each row is wider, so the same number per row gives larger thumbnails and fewer rows.

### Photos from an iPhone

iPhone photos are often HEIC files. If your browser can't open them, convert them first with
[HEIC to JPG](/heic-to-jpg), then add the JPGs. Very large photos work, but a contact sheet only needs small thumbnails,
so if you are making sheets of hundreds of big photos on a phone, shrinking them first with
[Resize Image](/resize-image) makes it faster.

## Making a contact sheet in Photoshop, Lightroom or Bridge

If you already pay for Adobe's apps, they can make contact sheets too:

- **Photoshop:** File › Automate › Contact Sheet II. It places a folder of images on a new document.
- **Lightroom Classic:** the Print module, with a grid layout such as one of the contact sheet templates.
- **Bridge:** the Output workspace, which saves a PDF contact sheet of the selected photos.

They are worth using if your photos are already organised there. If not, or if you don't have a subscription, a free
tool gives the same result for printing and sharing.

## Tips for a useful contact sheet

- **Put the photos in order first.** Shooting order tells the story of a session; for a client, grouping similar
  shots together makes choosing easier.
- **Keep file names on.** A client can write "IMG_2041 and IMG_2057" in an email, and you find them immediately.
- **Add a title.** A date, place or client name on each page saves confusion when sheets are printed and stacked.
- **Print at actual size.** In the print dialog, choose "Actual size" or 100%, not "Fit to page", so the margins come out
  as intended.
- **Send the PDF, not separate images.** One PDF with page numbers is easier to read and comment on than a folder of
  pictures.
